# A buffer under perturbation

Consider a closed teaching model containing a weak acid `HA` and its conjugate base `A-`. A small amount of strong acid adds protons to the model.

## Make a prediction

:::{callout}
:kind: transfer
:id: transfer-buffer-response

Predict which buffer component reacts with most of the added proton. Explain the response using both conjugate-pair language and a particle-level account. State one limit of the model.
:::

## Model response

:::{equation}
:label: equation-buffer-response

A^- + H^+ \rightarrow HA
:::

The conjugate base consumes added proton to form the weak acid. This explains resistance to a sudden pH change; it does not mean pH is fixed or that buffer capacity is unlimited.

## Where resistance ends

:::{figure} ../assets/buffer-titration.svg
:alt: A weak-acid titration curve flattens into a plateau centred on the pKa, then climbs steeply once the buffering region has been passed.
:longdesc: pH is plotted against the volume of strong base added to a weak acid. The curve climbs steeply from the origin, flattens into a plateau centred on the pKa, which is marked by a horizontal dotted line, and then climbs steeply again. A box drawn around the plateau is labelled buffering region. Within that box a given volume of base moves the pH only slightly; beyond it the same volume moves the pH a great deal.
:::

The plateau is the same response plotted against something a bench measures. Its width is what buffer capacity names, and its far edge is the point where the conjugate base has been spent, after which each further addition of base acts on an unbuffered solution. Read the two claims of the previous paragraph off the picture: the flat stretch is the resistance, and the climb that follows it is the limit.
