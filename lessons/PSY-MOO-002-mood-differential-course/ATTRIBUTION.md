## Terminology continuity

The psychiatry terminology ledger holds six terms, all owned by PSY-WEL-001, and this lesson defines none of them. Three further decisions were made against the wider corpus:

- **mood** — the glossary entry carries an `alignment` block declaring `adopt` from PSY-INT-004, which the psychiatry block assigns ownership of the mood and affect distinction and which is not yet authored. This lesson uses only the sustained-state sense and does not introduce *affect* as a technical term, so the not-yet-written lesson can define the pair without inheriting a second sense from here.
- **depression** — the everyday word for feeling low and the clinical word for a syndrome collide. The collision is named in one clause where the word first appears in the first scene, and the glossary entry records that what the syndrome consists of belongs to PSY-250.01 rather than here.
- **leverage** — an earlier draft used *leverage* for the property this lesson now calls *weight*. `PREM-STA-011` already owns *leverage* in its regression sense, so the term was renamed rather than given a third meaning across the corpus. No `alignment` block was needed after the rename.

## Source use and rights

Eleven sources support the factual claims. Every one was used for facts only, followed by independent instructional structure and original expression; no source's wording, table, figure, list order, case, or section structure was reproduced or closely paraphrased. Complete locators, use records, rights evidence, and dated agent-access checks appear in `references.json`.

- Regeer and colleagues, *Low self-recognition and awareness of past hypomanic and manic episodes in the general population* (`source-regeer`): recognition of past elevated versus depressive episodes among 40 adults with a research diagnosis of bipolar disorder, and the reasons non-recognisers gave.
- O'Donovan and Alda, *Depression Preceding Diagnosis of Bipolar Disorder* (`source-odonovan-alda`): family history as the best-replicated predictor of a bipolar trajectory, the features travelling with it, the reported interval to diagnosis, and recall bias in reports of past episodes.
- Ghaemi and colleagues, *Clinical research diagnostic criteria for bipolar illness (CRDC-BP)* (`source-ghaemi-crdc`): the origin and contested empirical status of the four-day hypomania duration convention, the proposed two-day minimum, and the recorded counter-argument about widened diagnosis.
- Gilman, Dupuy and Perlis, *Prospective, population-based study of the transition from major depressive disorder to bipolar disorder* (`source-gilman-nesarc`): the transition proportion over about three years in 6,214 adults, and the authors' stated limitation that family history of mania was not measured.
- Shivani, Goldsmith and Anthenelli, *Alcoholism and Psychiatric Disorders: Diagnostic Challenges* (`source-shivani`): chronology as the discriminator between independent and alcohol-related presentations, and the two-to-four-week observation period.
- Stein and Friedmann, *Disturbed Sleep and Its Relationship to Alcohol Use* (`source-stein-friedmann`): the shape of the night under evening alcohol, rebound arousal after blood alcohol falls, loss of the hypnotic effect with regular use, and persistence of sleep disturbance into abstinence.
- Zamwar and Muneshwar, *Epidemiology, Types, Causes, Clinical Presentation, Diagnosis, and Treatment of Hypothyroidism* (`source-zamwar`): the usual presenting features, their non-specificity, the usual direction of weight change, and the tests that establish the diagnosis.
- Samuels, *Subclinical Hypothyroidism and Depression: Is There a Link?* (`source-samuels`): the frequency of mildly raised thyroid-stimulating hormone with normal free thyroxine in tested depressed patients and the unestablished causal link with depression.
- Rogers and Pies, *General Medical Drugs Associated with Depression* (`source-rogers-pies`): the breadth of drug classes associated with depressive symptoms, the difference between association and cause, and the temporal arguments that carry weight.
- Janshen and Eisma, *Bidirectional associations between prolonged grief symptoms and depressive, anxiety, and posttraumatic stress symptoms* (`source-janshen-eisma`): prolonged grief as descriptively distinct from depression and post-traumatic stress while frequently co-occurring, and the bidirectional longitudinal findings.
- Walker and colleagues, *Circadian rhythm disruption and mental health* (`source-walker`): the meta-analytic figure for night-shift work and depression, the mixed findings for major depressive disorder specifically, and the unresolved direction of causation.

Every source was reached through `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/`, which the project source-access ledger records as allowed on 2026-07-25 because the host robots file carries an explicit `Allow: /articles/`. That entry was inside the ledger's stated 90-day recheck window at the time of access, so no re-check was required and no ledger entry needed adding: no domain outside the ledger was opened during this run. The legacy `www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles` route, which the ledger records as restricted, was not used. No login, paywall, rate limit, or technical control was bypassed for any source.

Three of the eleven sources display a publisher copyright rather than an open licence (`source-gilman-nesarc`, `source-samuels`, `source-rogers-pies`), and one is a publisher-copyright author manuscript (`source-stein-friedmann`). All four are cited for facts only under original expression, which is the basis recorded for them in `references.json`; none of their expression, media, or data is reused.

## Deliberate omissions and open questions for review

- **The neurologic branch carries no source of its own.** One candidate review of depression in neurological disorders was opened and its full text could not be read through the permitted route, so nothing was cited from it. The lesson therefore teaches how to *place* a neurologic alternative on a differential and how to tell a countered one from an unassessed one, and asserts no clinical fact about which neurological conditions produce depressive presentations. A reviewer who wants that fact taught should ask for a verifiable source rather than accept an unsourced list.
- **No epidemiological claim is made about first-onset depression in later life**, although the transfer case turns on a late first presentation. The transfer reasoning runs on chronology and channel quality, which the lesson has already established, rather than on an unsourced prior probability.
- **The hypomania duration convention is left unresolved on purpose.** Both readings are cited and neither is adopted.
- Every claim carries an `uncertainty` field naming the specific limit of its source, including small sample size, single-country sampling, review dates, and the interest the CRDC-BP authors have in the question they are answering.
