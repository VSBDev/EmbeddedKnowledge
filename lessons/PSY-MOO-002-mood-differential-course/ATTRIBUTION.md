## Terminology continuity

The psychiatry terminology ledger holds six terms, all owned by PSY-WEL-001, and this lesson defines none of them. Three further decisions were made against the wider corpus:

- **mood** — the glossary entry carries an `alignment` block declaring `adopt` from PSY-INT-004, which the psychiatry block assigns ownership of the mood and affect distinction and which is not yet authored. This lesson uses only the sustained-state sense and does not introduce *affect* as a technical term, so the not-yet-written lesson can define the pair without inheriting a second sense from here.
- **depression** — the everyday word for feeling low and the clinical word for a syndrome collide. The collision is named in one clause where the word first appears in the first scene, and the glossary entry records that what the syndrome consists of belongs to PSY-250.01 rather than here.
- **leverage** — an earlier draft used *leverage* for the property this lesson now calls *weight*. `PREM-STA-011` already owns *leverage* in its regression sense, so the term was renamed rather than given a third meaning across the corpus. No `alignment` block was needed after the rename.

## What changed in 0.1.1, finding by finding

Version 0.1.1 answers two role-isolated reviews of the 0.1.0 candidate: an academic review returning request-changes with seven findings, and a learning-design review returning approve with one minor finding that independently corroborated academic finding 0. **Academic 0, the four-state taxonomy (major).** The taxonomy was removed rather than repaired. `content/020-weight-and-channel.md` now teaches three readings that are taken separately for every alternative: what the record says in both directions, how much of the alternative was actually asked about, and what would close the remainder, distinguishing a part that further conversation can settle from one needing an informant, a document, a test, or a period of observation. R.'s thyroid alternative is used in that scene as the case that defeats a single label, being supported and countered at once with an outstanding part that neither piece of evidence settles. The same scene now states that an alternative nobody asked about holds no position on the list at all, which repairs the implicit ranking the reviewer identified. The change was propagated to the first objective and the third objective in `lesson.json`, to the learner-facing objectives in `content/010-two-readings.md`, to the completion table and the nine-row ranking table in `content/050-the-rest-of-the-list.md`, which now carries columns for what speaks for, what speaks against, what was not asked, and what would close the rest, to the model note and the closing retrieval check in `content/070-clinical-wrap-up.md`, to the transfer prompts in `content/060-transfer-a-different-file.md`, to `glossary.json` at `term-differential` and `term-unassessed-alternative`, and to every assessment item and rubric in `assessment.json`. The recovery route in the wrap-up was rewritten around the same three readings.

**Learning-design 4, the undefined fifth label "Not entered" (minor).** Resolved by the same rewrite instead of by a relabel. With no exclusive states left, the medication alternative in the scene-5 table and in the scene-7 note is now described directly: nothing speaks for it, nothing speaks against it, the whole medication list is unasked, and one request would close it.

**Academic 5, the abstinence safety gap (major, highest priority).** `content/040-the-drink-and-the-waking.md` now opens the alcohol reasoning with a dedicated safety callout, `callout-withdrawal-risk`, placed before the misconception block so the boundary is read first. It states that stopping abruptly in physical dependence can precipitate a withdrawal syndrome whose severe forms include seizures and delirium, that risk is assessed first from the pattern of drinking, the severity of dependence, previous withdrawal episodes and their complications, and comorbidity, and that withdrawal is medically assisted with round-the-clock assessment and monitoring where risk is found. It states explicitly that R.'s file records no quantity, no dependence severity, no previous withdrawal and no comorbidity, that nothing in it supports proposing that R. stop drinking, and that the lesson does not. The two-to-four-week interval is now framed throughout as a rule for reading a period of abstinence that has already occurred safely, and the recheck cases, the wrap-up note, the recovery route, and the feedback on `item-chronology-choice` all carry that qualification. `claim-substance-chronology-discriminator` was rewritten to the same effect, its scope narrowed, and a new `claim-withdrawal-risk-precedes-abstinence` added at `risk: safety-critical` on two new sources, `source-stewart-swain` and `source-steel-ats`. A glossary entry, `term-alcohol-withdrawal`, was added and marks the scope boundary: the lesson names the assessment that precedes an abstinence interval and does not teach withdrawal management.

**Academic 1, the unipolar shortcut in the answer key (major).** The key for `item-rank-a-new-file` no longer treats a depressive syndrome as evidence for a unipolar course. It now states that a seven-month depressive picture occurs in both course alternatives and separates neither, that unipolar stays viable only as far as the enquiry into past elevated or activated episodes has been adequate, that K.'s enquiry consists of one undated recollection from K. alone, and that this file cannot place either course alternative above the other. The rubric criterion was replaced by `criterion-course-alternatives-not-shortcut`, which explicitly withholds the point from a response reading "unipolar: supported, on the seven-month picture". The same shortcut was removed from prose in `content/050-the-rest-of-the-list.md`, where the ranking table's unipolar row and the paragraph beneath it were rewritten, and in `content/070-clinical-wrap-up.md`, where "A unipolar depressive course fits the present picture and is the leading reading on current evidence" was replaced by a statement that the depressive presentation is established and the course is open. `claim-cross-sectional-underdetermines-course` now maps to `scene-rest-of-the-list` as well, and that scene's source note carries it.

**Academic 3, collateral overstated (major).** The words best, only and cannot were removed from `content/060-transfer-a-different-file.md`. Its second discussion point now asks both: T. is named as the only source for the inside of the last nine months and is asked directly about past elevated or activated periods, with the observation that being depressed changes what some answers are worth without removing his ability to give them; the daughter is asked, with T.'s consent, for what he is worst placed to give, and her account is bounded by which periods she witnessed, her own recall across the same years, and her relationship to him. **Academic 2, facts not in the stem (major).** The key for `item-weight-and-channel-of-one-item` no longer converts K.'s remembered spell into an elevated or activated change from usual state. ## Source use and rights

Fifteen sources support the factual claims. Every one was used for facts only, followed by independent instructional structure and original expression; no source's wording, table, figure, list order, case, or section structure was reproduced or closely paraphrased. Complete locators, use records, rights evidence, and dated agent-access checks appear in `references.json`.

- Regeer and colleagues, *Low self-recognition and awareness of past hypomanic and manic episodes in the general population* (`source-regeer`): recognition of past elevated versus depressive episodes among 40 adults with a research diagnosis of bipolar disorder, and the reasons non-recognisers gave.
- O'Donovan and Alda, *Depression Preceding Diagnosis of Bipolar Disorder* (`source-odonovan-alda`): family history as the best-replicated predictor of a bipolar trajectory, the features travelling with it, the reported interval to diagnosis, and recall bias in reports of past episodes.
- Ghaemi and colleagues, *Clinical research diagnostic criteria for bipolar illness (CRDC-BP)* (`source-ghaemi-crdc`): the origin and contested empirical status of the four-day hypomania duration convention, the proposed two-day minimum, and the recorded counter-argument about widened diagnosis.
- Gilman, Dupuy and Perlis, *Prospective, population-based study of the transition from major depressive disorder to bipolar disorder* (`source-gilman-nesarc`): the transition proportion over about three years in 6,214 adults, and the authors' stated limitation that family history of mania was not measured.
- Shivani, Goldsmith and Anthenelli, *Alcoholism and Psychiatric Disorders: Diagnostic Challenges* (`source-shivani`): chronology as the discriminator between independent and alcohol-related presentations, and the two-to-four-week interval used to read a period of abstinence. The review addresses the diagnostic use of such an interval and says nothing about achieving abstinence safely, which is why the withdrawal qualification is carried on separate sources.
- Stein and Friedmann, *Disturbed Sleep and Its Relationship to Alcohol Use* (`source-stein-friedmann`): the shape of the night under evening alcohol, rebound arousal after blood alcohol falls, loss of the hypnotic effect with regular use, and persistence of sleep disturbance into abstinence.
- Zamwar and Muneshwar, *Epidemiology, Types, Causes, Clinical Presentation, Diagnosis, and Treatment of Hypothyroidism* (`source-zamwar`): the usual presenting features, their non-specificity, the usual direction of weight change, and the tests that establish the diagnosis.
- Samuels, *Subclinical Hypothyroidism and Depression: Is There a Link?* (`source-samuels`): the frequency of mildly raised thyroid-stimulating hormone with normal free thyroxine in tested depressed patients and the unestablished causal link with depression.
- Rogers and Pies, *General Medical Drugs Associated with Depression* (`source-rogers-pies`): the breadth of drug classes associated with depressive symptoms, the difference between association and cause, and the temporal arguments that carry weight. No specific drug is now named on this source alone.
- Stewart and Swain, *Assessment and management of alcohol dependence and withdrawal in the acute hospital: concise guidance* (`source-stewart-swain`): that withdrawal risk is assessed before and during any reduction in drinking, that severe or impending severe withdrawal is a reason to admit with delirium tremens and seizures as absolute criteria, the lower threshold for vulnerable groups, and the requirement for settings with round-the-clock assessment and monitoring.
- Steel and colleagues, *Research Needs for Inpatient Management of Severe Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome: An Official American Thoracic Society Research Statement* (`source-steel-ats`): severe withdrawal as a state of central nervous system hyperexcitation following reduction or cessation of alcohol, and the features that mark highest risk. None of the statement's management content is used.
- Leissner and colleagues, *Short- and long-term effects of beta-blockers on symptoms of anxiety and depression in patients with myocardial infarction and preserved left ventricular function* (`source-leissner-reduce-ami`): the REDUCE-AMI quality-of-life sub-study design, the agents allocated, the direction and approximate size of the depressive-symptom difference at both follow-ups, the absence of an anxiety effect, and the authors' own limitations.
- Molero and colleagues, *Associations between β-blockers and psychiatric and behavioural outcomes: A population-based cohort study of 1.4 million individuals in Sweden* (`source-molero-betablockers`): the within-individual design and cohort size, the hazard ratio for hospitalisation for depressive disorders during treatment periods, the agent-level direction for atenolol and metoprolol, and the register and confounding limits.
- Janshen and Eisma, *Bidirectional associations between prolonged grief symptoms and depressive, anxiety, and posttraumatic stress symptoms* (`source-janshen-eisma`): prolonged grief as descriptively distinct from depression and post-traumatic stress while frequently co-occurring, and the bidirectional longitudinal findings.
- Walker and colleagues, *Circadian rhythm disruption and mental health* (`source-walker`): the meta-analytic figure for night-shift work and depression, the mixed findings for major depressive disorder specifically, and the unresolved direction of causation.

Every source was reached through `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/`, which the project source-access ledger records as allowed on 2026-07-25 because the host robots file carries an explicit `Allow: /articles/`. That entry was inside the ledger's stated 90-day recheck window at the time of access, so no re-check of it was required. The legacy `www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles` route, which the ledger records as restricted, was not used. No login, paywall, rate limit, or technical control was bypassed for any source.

Two domains outside the ledger were checked during the 0.1.1 revision and both were appended to `site/agent/source-access-ledger.json` as `excluded`, so the next author inherits the finding. `www.ahajournals.org` was checked while looking for Riemer and colleagues 2021 in *Hypertension*, the randomised-trial meta-analysis of psychiatric adverse events during beta-blocker therapy that academic finding 4 alludes to; its full text is free on that host and absent from PMC, but the host robots file carries `Disallow: /` for GPTBot, which was read as the publisher prohibiting agent ingestion. `bpspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com` was checked while looking for a 2025 *British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology* systematic review of beta blockers and neuropsychiatric outcomes; it carries `Disallow: /` for both GPTBot and Google-Extended. Neither article was opened and nothing was cited from either. The beta-blocker point is therefore carried on the two PMC-hosted studies listed above, which between them supply the randomised and the agent-level evidence the finding asked for, and the lesson states the residual uncertainty instead of claiming a settled null.

Five of the fifteen sources display a publisher or society copyright rather than an open licence (`source-gilman-nesarc`, `source-samuels`, `source-rogers-pies`, `source-stewart-swain`, `source-steel-ats`), and one is a publisher-copyright author manuscript (`source-stein-friedmann`). All six are cited for facts only under original expression, which is the basis recorded for them in `references.json`; none of their expression, media, or data is reused. No instrument, scale item, scoring band, admission algorithm, or diagnostic-manual text is reproduced from any of them.

## Deliberate omissions and open questions for review

- **The neurologic branch carries no source of its own.** One candidate review of depression in neurological disorders was opened and its full text could not be read through the permitted route, so nothing was cited from it. The lesson therefore teaches how to *place* a neurologic alternative on a differential and how to tell an alternative argued against from one nobody asked about, and asserts no clinical fact about which neurological conditions produce depressive presentations. A reviewer who wants that fact taught should ask for a verifiable source rather than accept an unsourced list.
- **The beta-blocker evidence is stated as unsettled at a small magnitude, not as a null.** The randomised-trial meta-analysis academic finding 4 points to sits on a host this run recorded as excluded, so the class question is carried on a randomised sub-study and a within-individual register cohort that disagree in direction while agreeing that any effect is small. A reviewer with access to the meta-analysis through a permitted route may wish to say whether adding it would change how the transfer case reads.
- **No claim is made about beta-blocker agents that neither cited study reported separately.** The agent-level statement is limited to metoprolol, bisoprolol and atenolol, and the lipophilicity question raised elsewhere in that literature is not taught here.
- **No epidemiological claim is made about first-onset depression in later life**, although the transfer case turns on a late first presentation. The transfer reasoning runs on chronology and channel quality, which the lesson has already established, rather than on an unsourced prior probability.
- **The hypomania duration convention is left unresolved on purpose.** Both readings are cited and neither is adopted.
- Every claim carries an `uncertainty` field naming the specific limit of its source, including small sample size, single-country sampling, review dates, and the interest the CRDC-BP authors have in the question they are answering.

## Boundaries and safety
