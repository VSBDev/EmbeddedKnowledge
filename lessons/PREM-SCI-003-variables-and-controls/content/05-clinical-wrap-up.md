# Clinical wrap-up: audit the diabetes comparison

This is how the dinner-timing prediction turns into variables a comparison can actually hold steady.

**Teaching example—not medical advice.** This case is invented for reasoning practice. It does not claim that dinner timing truly affects glucose and must not be used to guide care.

PREM-SCI-002's prediction, in one line, was that for the fictional adult with type 2 diabetes, the next-morning fasting glucose reading would be higher after the later dinner than after the earlier dinner.

## Name the two main jobs

- **Independent variable:** dinner timing, with earlier and later as its two levels. Its job is to mark the factor set differently in the comparison.
- **Dependent variable:** the next-morning fasting glucose reading. Its job is to provide the response recorded after each dinner-timing condition.

These labels organize the comparison. They do not show that the prediction is true.

## Hold conditions alike without confusing them with a control condition

In this invented comparison, the team could decide to hold these variables alike across the earlier- and later-dinner conditions:

- total evening carbohydrate;
- sleep time; and
- the glucose meter used.

Those are **controlled variables** when the team deliberately keeps them alike across both dinner-timing levels. Each is a feature held steady within the main comparison.

A **control condition** has a different job: it is an extra comparison condition included to provide a baseline, a known response, or another stated check. The earlier-dinner condition is one level of the independent variable; it is not automatically a positive control or a negative control. Likewise, the shared glucose meter is a controlled variable, not a control condition.

## Test one candidate confounder

Add one stipulated detail to the teaching case: evenings with earlier dinners also include more evening physical activity than evenings with later dinners. For this invented exercise, assume that the activity schedule is separate from dinner timing and could change the next-morning fasting glucose reading.

Evening physical activity is then a candidate **confounder** because it passes all three checks:

1. **Separate rival factor:** the activity schedule is assigned separately; it is not a step through which dinner timing is proposed to produce the reading.
2. **Tracks the independent variable:** more activity occurs with the earlier-dinner condition and less with the later-dinner condition.
3. **Could affect the dependent variable:** the case stipulates that the activity difference could change the next-morning fasting glucose reading.

The label identifies an ambiguity in the supplied comparison. It does not choose a study design or adjust the result.

## Keep control checks in their own role

If the fictional comparison also supplied a **positive-control condition** defined in advance to produce a known meter response, a successful result would show that the setup can reveal that response. If it supplied a **negative-control condition** defined in advance to remain at a baseline response, that result would show whether the setup produced a response where none was expected. These would be extra comparison conditions, not variables held alike across dinner times.

The role audit now has a narrow conclusion: dinner timing is the factor compared, next-morning fasting glucose is the response recorded, the listed conditions are held alike, and the stipulated activity pattern is a rival factor that travels with dinner timing.
