# Three passes, with the support falling away

Work each pass before opening its feedback. The first supplies the steps, the second supplies only the test, and the third supplies nothing.

## First pass: an instrument you can hold

A hand lens magnifies about ten times. Assume for now that magnifying an object by a factor $M$ makes it subtend $M$ times the angle at your eye, so the smallest detail you can separate through it is your unaided threshold divided by $M$.

:::{check}
:id: check-hand-lens
:kind: practice

1. Your unaided threshold is about 100 µm. What is the smallest detail a ten-times hand lens lets you separate?
2. Would that instrument have settled the question of whether gut lining is made of cells? Answer with the factor, not with a yes or a no.
3. If magnification alone set the limit, what magnification would resolve the 2 nm width of the DNA double helix?
:::

### Feedback after your attempt

100 µm divided by 10 gives about 10 µm. A colonic cell is 10 to 20 µm, so a hand lens lands right on the boundary. You might see that a surface is granular; you would not reliably separate one cell from its neighbour, and you certainly could not describe what a cell is. That fits the history: what Hooke used was better than a hand lens, and even his instruments named the walls and not the contents.

For the third question, 100 µm divided by 2 nm is 50,000, so magnification alone would suggest fifty thousand times. That number does not solve the problem, and the reason is worth being clear about. A light microscope's floor of about 0.25 µm is a spatial-resolution limit, not a magnification limit. An object narrower than that floor appears at the width of the point-spread function however much you enlarge the image, and two objects closer together than the floor are not distinguished as two. Enlarging past the limit produces a larger blur and no finer spatial detail. That does not make every sub-resolution object undetectable: a selective label or strong contrast may still let an observer detect or localise its signal while its width, shape, or close neighbours remain unresolved. Magnification, resolution, detection and localisation are related but different quantities.

## Second pass: emergent or not

Use both halves of the test. Look for the property in one cell. Then take the arrangement apart while keeping every cell alive and see whether the property survives.

:::{check}
:id: check-classify-emergence
:kind: practice

Classify each as a property of a single cell or a property of the organisation. Give the one-line reason.

1. A cell keeps a boundary between its inside and the fluid around it.
2. The lining keeps the contents of the colon out of the tissue underneath.
3. A cell manufactures a mucin protein.
4. A layer of mucus covers the surface of the epithelium.
5. A cell's behaviour depends on how far it is from the crypt base.
6. A cell divides in two.
7. Much of the rapidly renewed lining turns over on a three-to-five-day timescale while tissue-level coverage remains continuous.
:::

### Feedback after your attempt

Items 1, 3, and 6 are properties of a single cell. Each one is present in a cell on its own and each survives dissociating the crypt, so both halves of the test say the same thing.

Items 2, 4, 5, and 7 are properties of the organisation. Item 2 is continuous tissue-level coverage, which no single patch supplies; that does not deny the transcellular permeability of an individual cell or the paracellular permeability governed by junctions. Item 4 needs many secreting cells over an area, and a mucin protein made by one cell is not a layer. Item 5 is the sharpest case: distance from the base is a relation to other cells, so a dissociated cell has no such distance and there is nothing left for its behaviour to depend on. Item 7 needs cells doing different jobs in different places at the same time, which one cell cannot do at any moment. The three-to-five-day figure describes rapidly renewed bulk at population level, not synchronous replacement of every crypt cell: lifetimes vary by cell type and self-renewing lineages persist.

If you called item 4 a single-cell property, reread the difference between item 3 and item 4. Making the molecule and covering the tissue are separate claims, and the lesson's whole point sits in the gap between them.

## Third pass: no support

:::{check}
:id: check-design-the-observation
:kind: practice

A group wants to watch one cell travel from the base of a human colonic crypt to the surface, in a living person, using an instrument that delivers about 30 µm of spatial resolution to the observer. Work only within the lesson's idealised scale-and-diffraction model.

Say which listed dimensions that instrument resolves and which it does not. Then decide whether spatial resolution alone determines if one selectively labelled cell can be followed. Name the additional conditions the model leaves unspecified. Finally, state the conditional resolution needed to distinguish adjacent unlabelled cells about one cell width apart when contrast is adequate, say what the conventional light-microscope floor does and does not establish about feasibility, and estimate how long the observation would have to run.
:::

### Feedback after your attempt

For a surface view, crypt depth is the wrong dimension. The lesson's simplified circular model gives an opening about 70 µm across, so a 30 µm spatial limit is fine enough to resolve that width in principle when contrast is adequate. The model supplies no opening-to-opening separation, however, so it does not license a claim about resolving neighbouring openings, their spacing, or their density. Individual cells are 10 to 20 µm across, which is finer than 30 µm, so their boundaries, widths and close neighbours are not resolved.

That spatial comparison does not determine whether one selectively labelled cell can be followed. A sub-resolution label may still be detected and localised, while maintaining the identity of one cell over time also depends on label specificity, contrast, sampling rate, signal stability, motion and the separation from competing signals. If the task instead requires distinguishing adjacent unlabelled cells about 10 µm apart with adequate contrast, the conditional spatial requirement is finer than about 10 µm, with more margin needed to resolve shape.

Within this idealised model, the conventional light-microscope diffraction floor is not the limiting term for that conditional two-cell discrimination: 0.25 µm is roughly forty times finer than 10 µm. That comparison does **not** establish that engineering is the only obstacle or that observation in a living person is feasible. The exercise supplies no evidence about optical access, labelling, tissue effects, physiological motion, safety, or tracking stability, so it cannot decide those questions. Extending the measured lower-crypt migration speed over the whole modelled length gives an observation time of roughly three to seven days; the three-to-five-day bulk renewal interval is a consistency check, not proof that every cell completes the journey on one schedule. A six-hour or single-session view would capture only a small part of the process.

## Where to go next

- Every pass right: continue to the clinical wrap-up.
- Trouble in the first pass: the fault is usually treating magnification, resolution and detection as one quantity. Reread the last paragraph of that feedback, then say aloud what remains unresolved below 0.25 µm and what a strong label may still let you detect or localise.
- Trouble in the second pass: run both halves of the test in writing every time, in that order. Most errors come from stopping after the first half.
- Trouble in the third pass: rebuild the ladder from the second scene on paper, largest at the top, and put the instrument's 30 µm on it before answering anything.

## Accessibility and alternatives

Every task here can be answered in prose, in a list, or in a table, whichever suits you, and typed, dictated, or entered through assistive technology. The two figures carry long descriptions that state everything the picture is being used for: the scale-ladder diagram is a list of five sizes with the factor between each pair, and the micrograph's description gives the striation count against the scale bar and the conclusion drawn from it, so no task depends on seeing either one. All arithmetic is single-step and can be done with a calculator, on paper, or in your head; the answers are ranges, and an estimate landing anywhere inside the stated range is correct. Nothing in this lesson depends on colour, position on a page, dragging, sound, motion, or speed, and no task asks about your own body, health, medication, or test results.

## Recovery route

If the small-angle step in the worked example was where you came unstuck, the arithmetic is one multiplication and the difficulty is almost always the unit conversion. Return to [Units and dimensional analysis (PREM-QNT-002)](https://embeddedknowledge.io/premed/lessons/read/?lesson=PREM-QNT-002) and check that you can carry micrometres, millimetres, and radians through a product without losing track of which is which.

If comparing $10^{13}$ with $10^{-6}$ felt unfamiliar, go back to [Powers, roots, and scale (PREM-QNT-005)](https://embeddedknowledge.io/premed/lessons/read/?lesson=PREM-QNT-005). This lesson uses orders of magnitude constantly and never re-teaches them.

If *theory* still reads as a downgrade, the repair is procedural. For any claim you meet, write down what reproducible observation would count strongly against it and why that observation falls inside the claim's stated scope. Do that for three claims in this lesson before continuing.

If emergence still feels like a special ingredient, take the dissociation test literally. Name the property, imagine every cell alive in a tube, and ask what is missing. What is missing is always an arrangement, and never a substance.

If the crypt itself is not yet a clear picture, redraw it: a tube sunk into the wall, dividing cells at the bottom, cells changing job as they rise, working cells at the top, and cells leaving at the surface. If you can draw that from memory you have what the rest of this block builds on. If it is still not clear after another reading, ask your course text or instructor instead of pressing on, because every remaining lesson in the module returns to this structure.
