# "Only a theory": the word that will trip you

Somebody in your year will eventually say that cell theory is only a theory. It is worth having the answer ready, because the same move gets made about germ theory, about evolution, and about anything a clinician would rather not accept.

:::{check}
:id: check-elicit-theory-sense
:kind: retrieval

Before reading on, commit to two answers in writing.

1. What do you take the word *theory* to mean in ordinary speech? Give an example sentence.
2. Is "cell theory is only a theory" a criticism? If so, of what exactly?
:::

## The everyday meaning, stated fairly

In ordinary English a theory is a private guess. "I have a theory about why the ward is short-staffed" announces an idea somebody has formed ahead of the evidence, offered without much weight behind it and expected to be replaced when better information turns up. That usage is not sloppy; it is a real and useful sense of the word, and it is the sense almost every learner brings to a biology course.

Carried into science, it produces a predictable reading: *cell theory* names somebody's idea about cells, *germ theory* names somebody's idea about infection, and each sits alongside rival ideas of similar standing, waiting for a decisive experiment.

:::{misconception}
:id: misconception-theory-means-guess

**The model to test.** A scientific theory is a proposal awaiting confirmation, comparable in standing to any other proposal in the field, and a single awkward observation could sink it.

**What that model cannot explain.** Look at what actually happens in a hospital. A pathologist receiving a colon biopsy does not begin by establishing that the tissue is made of cells. That the specimen is cellular, that its cells came from earlier cells, and that its disease can be read from those cells are the assumptions the whole examination rests on. If cell theory were a live proposal awaiting confirmation, an entire diagnostic discipline would be resting on an untested guess, and the guess would have failed somewhere in a hundred and sixty years of specimens. Nothing about that picture matches how the work is done or what it has produced.

**The repair.** In science the word marks the opposite end of the scale from a guess. A theory is an explanatory account with stated assumptions and a declared domain of use, which has been put at risk of failure repeatedly across that domain and has not failed. Its authority comes from what it has withstood. Calling something a theory in this sense is the strongest claim a field makes, and it sits above a hypothesis, which is a single proposal still to be tested.

**Test the repair.** Ask, of any claim, what observation would have destroyed it and whether anyone went looking. A guess has no answer to the second half of that question. Cell theory has a long one.
:::

[Scientific models and their limits (PREM-SCI-010)](https://embeddedknowledge.io/premed/lessons/read/?lesson=PREM-SCI-010) already set the standard this course judges a scientific account by: stated assumptions, a domain in which it may be applied, and tests that could have gone the other way. This block adopts that standard for the word *theory* and does not restate it. What follows is the specific account and the specific tests.

## What cell theory actually asserts

Cell theory was established in Berlin in 1838 and 1839, by Matthias Schleiden working on plants and Theodor Schwann on animals, and its core proposition is that all tissues are composed of cells as a basic unit. To that, later work added that every cell arises from the division of a pre-existing cell. In 1858 Rudolf Virchow's *Cellularpathologie* drew the medical consequence: disease can be diagnosed by examining the cells in a patient's tissue, which is the basis of histological diagnosis to this day.

Historians who have looked closely find that the exact list of propositions varies between accounts and that the claims were argued over and refined for decades after 1839. One standard version has three parts: plants and animals are made of cells, cells carry all the attributes of life, and cells arise from division of pre-existing cells. Treat that three-part version as a summary of a settled position, not as a formula handed down whole.

## What would have destroyed it

The propositions are worth stating because each one was refusable, and each was genuinely at risk.

| Proposition | What would have refuted it | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tissues are built from cells as their basic unit | A tissue found to be built from something else, or from nothing divisible at all | Two centuries of microscopy across plants, animals, and human organs kept finding cells |
| Cells arise from division of pre-existing cells | A cell shown to condense out of non-cellular material | The rival accounts of cell formation from a formative fluid were investigated and did not survive |
| Cells carry the attributes of life | A tissue whose living properties were shown to sit outside its cells | The properties kept being traceable to cells and to their arrangement |

Each row is a place where the theory could have died and did not. That accumulation is what the word *theory* is reporting.

## What it does not assert

Being well established is not the same as being complete, and cell theory is silent about a great deal. It does not say what a cell is made of. It does not say how one works. It does not say why a lining is built from many small units instead of one large one, which is what the next scene answers. It fixed the unit of biological organisation and left everything about the unit's contents to a further century of instruments.

:::{check}
:id: check-recheck-theory-sense
:kind: retrieval

Sort these three statements. Which is a hypothesis, which is a theory in the scientific sense, and which is a guess in the everyday sense? Give your reason for each in one line.

1. Human tissues are built from cells, and every cell comes from a dividing cell.
2. The reason this patient's crypts look distorted is that the biopsy was taken during a flare.
3. I reckon crypts are shallower in older people.
:::

Statement 1 is a theory: it has a domain covering all human tissue, it has been at risk of refutation for well over a century, and diagnostic practice is built on it. Statement 2 is a hypothesis: a single proposed explanation for one specimen, which the notes and a repeat biopsy could support or overturn. Statement 3 is a guess in the everyday sense: an idea nobody has tested. Notice that the guess could become a hypothesis the moment somebody states how it would be measured and what would count against it, and that no amount of confidence turns a hypothesis into a theory. Only surviving the tests does that.
