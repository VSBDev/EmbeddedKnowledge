# Breaking one anchor

A tidy diagram invites a tidy error. Four attachments, drawn as one stack, joined to a shared cytoskeleton, all serving the integrity of the sheet. From there it is a short step to a prediction that feels obvious and is wrong.

:::{misconception}
:id: misconception-one-system

**The incorrect model.** The junctional complex is one integrated system. Break any structural member of it and the sheet degrades across the board: adhesion weakens, the barrier leaks, the cytoskeleton disorganises, the cells suffer.

**Why it is tempting.** It is how most built structures behave, and the parts genuinely are adjacent, interdependent in places, and often described together. The phrase "junctional complex" does real damage here by suggesting a single machine.

**What actually happens.** Delete the desmosome's link to keratin and almost nothing on that list occurs. One specific thing fails, and it is not on the list at all.

**The discriminating test.** Remove one anchor and check every function separately instead of checking the sheet as a whole. The experiment below does exactly that.
:::

## The experiment

Desmoplakin is the cytolinker from the previous scene: the protein that ties keratin filaments to the desmosome. Delete it and the desmosome loses its grip on keratin while everything else about the junction is left in place. That is an unusually clean cut, and it was made.

:::{investigation}
:id: investigation-desmoplakin-deletion

**Setup.** Desmoplakin was conditionally deleted in the intestinal epithelium of mice, using a Villin-driven Cre to restrict the deletion to that tissue. Wild-type littermates served as controls.

**What failed.** Microvilli. They were significantly shortened, and they were often irregularly shaped and vesiculated.

**What did not fail.** Nearly everything else that was checked.

- Adherens junctions were intact: E-cadherin localisation was unchanged.
- Tight junctions were intact: ZO-1 localisation was unchanged. The authors report no changes in the apical junctional complex.
- The barrier held. A biotin tracer applied to the tissue did not leak along the lateral membranes, and the authors state that desmoplakin is not required for barrier function of the intestinal epithelium.
- Keratin organisation was indistinguishable from wild type at the light-microscope level, and keratin stayed enriched in the apical region.
- Desmosomes themselves were still present, in their proper subapical location at each cell-to-cell junction. What they had lost was the attachment of keratin filaments to them.
- The other desmosomal proteins localised normally: plakoglobin and plakophilin 2 were unaffected.
- Microtubules were still arranged along the apicobasal axis.
- Every terminal web component examined was localised as in wild type, at comparable expression levels.

**The animals.** Indistinguishable from wild-type littermates. Viable, fertile, normal lifespan, normal growth rate, body weight and intestine length. No sign of the inflammation or bacterial infection that a compromised barrier would produce.

**The authors' reading.** They propose that losing keratin's anchorage to the desmosome weakens the terminal web, so that it can no longer sustain long microvilli, which exert force on the terminal web as they grow out. They offer a second possible route as well, that desmoplakin influences signalling to the actin cytoskeleton, and note the resemblance of the affected microvilli to calcium-treated brush borders, which would implicate villin's severing activity. They say this requires further investigation.

**Stated limits.** The authors cannot rule out that other proteins partially rescue keratin attachment to the desmosome, which would mean the cut was less clean than intended. The mechanism of apical keratin localisation remains incompletely understood. A worsening of the phenotype in older outbred mice was noticed and not pursued.
:::

## What the result licenses, and what it does not

The honest summary is narrow. Removing the keratin anchor removed a keratin-dependent function and left the functions that depend on other anchors alone.

Notice how well that fits the previous scene's map. Adhesion between these cells runs through E-cadherin and catenins to actin, and the seal runs through claudins and ZO proteins to actin. Neither route passes through desmoplakin or keratin, so neither route broke. The map predicted which functions were at risk, and the experiment agrees.

The failure that did occur travelled by an unexpected path, and this is the part worth remembering. Microvillus length is an apical, actin-based property, three scenes' worth of argument away from a lateral desmosome. The connection is the terminal web: keratin anchored at the desmosome contributes to the mat that the actin core bundles are planted in, and a growing microvillus pushes against that mat. Weaken the mat and the projection it supports cannot be held as long. Scene 1's structure and scene 4's structure are mechanically continuous, which no diagram of separate junctions would suggest.

Two cautions on how far this goes.

The proposed route from lost keratin anchorage to shortened microvilli is the authors' interpretation, offered alongside an alternative involving actin signalling, and they call for further work. Treat it as the best current reading of their own result and not as an established mechanism.

And this is mouse small intestine with a tissue-restricted deletion, in animals that were otherwise healthy for a normal lifespan. It shows what one anchor is load-bearing for under ordinary conditions. It does not show what happens to a colonic sheet under mechanical stress, which is the condition where a tension-bearing anchor would be expected to matter most, and which the experiment did not impose.

:::{check}
:id: check-predict-the-failure

Suppose a different experiment removed E-cadherin from these cells instead of desmoplakin.

Using the map from the previous scene, name one function you would expect to fail and one you would expect to survive, and in each case say which anchor your expectation runs through. Then say what evidence would be needed before you could report either expectation as a result.

**Answer.** Expect lateral adhesion between neighbours to fail, because E-cadherin is the adhesive molecule of the adherens junction and the route from it to actin runs through the catenins. Expect the desmosomal route to survive that particular loss, because desmogleins and desmocollins are different adhesion molecules reaching keratin through a different set of linkers. Those are the two anchors the map separates.

Two cautions belong with the prediction. The junctions are not independent in practice: the desmosome review this lesson cites describes desmosomes influencing actin organisation and adhesion dynamics, so removing one adhesive system can change how another behaves. And a prediction is not a result. Reporting either of these would need the experiment done — the protein removed in this tissue, the named functions measured, and the localisation of the other junctions checked, which is exactly the set of observations the desmoplakin study above reports for its own deletion.
:::
