# Show that the nouns are under control

The mastery check runs on two studies you have not seen, so nothing can be answered from memory of the dinner-timing numbers. Five items, retakeable, feedback after the attempt. A calculator is enough; nothing here needs software.

Both studies below are invented for teaching. Neither describes a real trial, a real clinic, or a real patient, and neither supports any claim about pharmacist review or dietary programmes.

| Study | Participants | Difference in mean fasting glucose | Standard error | Two-sided *p* | 95 per cent interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A: pharmacist-led medication review | 100 | 7.2 mg/dL lower | 4.0 mg/dL | 0.072 | -0.6 to 15.0 mg/dL |
| B: workplace dietary programme | 2000 | 0.9 mg/dL lower | 0.30 mg/dL | 0.003 | 0.3 to 1.5 mg/dL |

Alpha was fixed at 0.05 in advance in both.

Both reported *p*-values and intervals use the same two-sided normal approximation as the dinner-timing worked example.

The five items ask you to:

1. state study A's null hypothesis and compute its test statistic from the difference and standard error;
2. choose the sentence that states correctly what study A's *p*-value is the probability of;
3. decide what study A's result does and does not establish about pharmacist review;
4. compare the two studies on effect size and on *p*, and say which comparison the *p*-values support;
5. say whether study B's interval and its *p*-value are two findings or one, and why.

Every item is scored on the reasoning as much as the arithmetic. A correct number attached to the wrong noun earns nothing, and the rubrics say so.

## Recovering from a miss

Each item's feedback names the idea that would have produced the right answer and points at the scene that teaches it.

A miss on item 1 sends you back to the division in scene 3. A miss on item 2 or item 4 sends you to scene 4, and specifically to the birth-month study, which is the shortest disproof of the inverted reading in this lesson. A miss on item 3 sends you to the reduced 30-person version of the dinner study, also in scene 4. A miss on item 5 sends you to the equivalence in scene 3 and the practice problem built on it.

Retake as often as you want. Nothing is timed.

## Spacing this so it survives

This material fades in a particular way. The definitions stay, and the grammar slips, so that six months from now the sentence comes out inverted while the formula stays correct. Three deliberate returns will hold it.

- **Within two days.** Write out the correct reading of *p* = 0.029 for the dinner-timing study from memory, in one sentence, starting with the word "if". Check that the noun after "probability of" refers to data.
- **Within two weeks.** Take any *p*-value you meet in reading, teaching, or a journal club. Write the correct sentence, then write the inverted one beside it, and say which one the paper's discussion actually used.
- **When lesson 09 arrives.** Before reading it, write down what you think a study of 60 people could have failed to detect. **PREM-STA-009** takes up exactly that question, and the reduced 30-person study in scene 4 is your first sight of it.

Lesson 04 asked you, at the end of its own mastery check, to predict what a *p*-value would turn out to be conditioned on. Go back and look at what you wrote.
