# Clinical wrap-up: audit an overnight glucose sketch

This is how a simple sketch of the overnight glucose response is used without being trusted too far.

**Illustrative teaching example—not medical advice.** Every person, comparison, relationship, symbol, and value in this scene is invented for model-audit practice. The sketch is not a validated clinical model, makes no claim about real glucose physiology, and must not guide care.

Return to the running question about earlier versus later dinner and next-morning fasting glucose. For this scene only, invent a direction score:

$S=k(L+C-F)$

In words, the sketch adds the invented dinner-lateness and carbohydrate-load scores, subtracts the invented fasting-interval score, and multiplies the result by an invented positive rate. Here $L$ is the dinner-lateness score, $C$ is the carbohydrate-load score, $F$ is the fasting-interval score, and $k$ is the made-up rate. A larger $S$ is stipulated to mean “predict a higher next-morning fasting glucose direction.” The symbols have no clinical units, and neither the equation nor its direction describes a real physiological relationship.

## Audit before use

- **Purpose:** organize expectations for the fictional earlier-versus-later dinner comparison.
- **Output:** a predicted direction—higher, lower, or unchanged—for the fictional next-morning fasting glucose reading, not a clinical value.
- **Inputs:** the invented scores for dinner time, carbohydrate load, and fasting interval.
- **Parameter:** the made-up positive rate $k$. It is not estimated from people or clinical data.
- **Assumptions:** the three score effects are linear and additive; one rate applies identically across the fictional adults; nothing relevant has been omitted; and no overnight medication condition changes or overrides the stipulated relation.
- **Scale:** one invented overnight interval and one next-morning reading.
- **Domain:** only the fictional adults with type 2 diabetes in this teaching comparison. The sketch does not cover type 1 diabetes, children, other nights, other outcomes, or individual care.

That audit is the useful part. The equation is only a teaching device for making its limits visible.

## Make and check one prediction

The supplied illustrative comparison gives the earlier- and later-dinner records the same carbohydrate-load and fasting-interval scores. Only the dinner-lateness score is larger for the later record.

The toy model therefore predicts a larger $S$, and thus a higher next-morning fasting glucose direction, for the later-dinner record. Now stipulate that the invented comparison also shows a higher reading after the later dinner. The directions agree.

That agreement checks one prediction in one invented comparison. It does not validate the sketch, establish a mechanism, or support a clinical conclusion.

## Stress-test an assumption and diagnose the failure

Change one assumption. For a different fictional adult, stipulate an overnight medication condition that keeps the next-morning reading unchanged across the earlier- and later-dinner records. Keep the invented carbohydrate-load and fasting-interval scores the same.

The sketch still predicts a higher direction after the later dinner because it has no medication input and uses the same positive $k$ for everyone. The illustrative record is unchanged instead. The failure is located in the assumptions and structure: the sketch omitted a condition that matters within this invented case and assumed one relation applies identically across people.

For this fictional use, **restrict** the model immediately: exclude the stipulated medication condition from its domain. If the purpose expands to include that condition, **revise** the structure before reuse so the condition and any person-specific response can be represented and tested. Merely **recalibrating** one common $k$ is not justified here, because changing a shared rate does not repair the omitted condition while preserving the earlier comparison. **Replace** the sketch if a tested alternative serves the expanded purpose better or if repeated invented failures show that this structure cannot.

The defensible response is therefore: restrict now, revise before broader use, and do not trust the directional match beyond the narrow invented comparison.
