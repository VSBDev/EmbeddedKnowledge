# Clinical wrap-up: eleven weeks later, has anything changed?

:::{callout}
:kind: boundary
:id: callout-teaching-boundary-wrapup

Teaching example, not medical advice. R. is fictional, and every number below was invented to make a point about records. Nothing here describes a real person's course, and nothing here is a basis for anyone's care.
:::

This is the question the description was built to answer, and it is a medical question before it is a clerical one. R. is seen again eleven weeks after the first appointment, by a clinician who was not there the first time. Suppose that appointment produces the following.

- The practice weighed R. in October: 73.5 kg, on the same scales as the July reading of 72 kg.
- R. says: "I still wake at four, but maybe three nights a week now. Twice I got back off."
- R. says: "I still haven't called anyone."
- R. says there have been no further write-ups.
- R. says the drinking is down to two evenings a week.
- Observed: answers came within a second or two; facial expression still changed little across the appointment; no coat this time.
- Not asked at this appointment: when the fatigue began, whether the interest in fishing has moved, or anything about concentration.
- Still no thyroid function result.

Now put the two possible baselines side by side and answer the question from each.

## From the referral's version

> 34-year-old, four-month history of depression. Poor sleep, weight loss, reduced function. ?bipolar.

Nothing can be said. Not "improved", not "unchanged", not "worse". "Poor sleep" has no direction, so three nights of early waking cannot be compared with whatever it stood for. "Weight loss" has no number, so 73.5 kg compares with nothing. "Reduced function" has no source and no specifics, so "no further write-ups" attaches to nothing. And "?bipolar" is still sitting in the record eleven weeks later, having never been evidence and never having been withdrawn.

The eleven weeks have produced real information and there is no instrument in the record to read it with.

## From the description you built

Five statements about change become available, and each one carries its own strength.

1. **Weight is up 1.5 kg between July and October**, both readings taken on the same practice scales. Two measurements from one instrument, so the comparison is as good as the instrument.
2. **Early waking has fallen from most nights to about three a week, and returns to sleep are now occurring where previously they were not.** Both the baseline and the follow-up are R.'s report, so this compares like with like. That is the condition that makes the comparison legitimate. Setting an observation against a self-report, or a nurse's account against a patient's, produces a difference that may belong to the source and not to the person.
3. **Response latency has fallen from about five seconds to one or two.** Observed both times, and observed by two different clinicians, so the comparison is weaker than the weight and should say so in the record.
4. **Facial expression changed little on both occasions.** One observed element has moved and another has not, and that is visible only because they were written on separate lines.
5. **Social contact is unchanged, and work has had no further written warnings**, both on R.'s account, with no employer's account at either time point.

Three elements cannot be compared at all. Energy has no baseline frequency or onset and was not re-asked. Interest and pleasure was not re-asked. Concentration was observed once and never tested. Two of those gaps were created at the first appointment, and one was created at the second. Both kinds are worth naming, because the fix is different: one is a writing problem and the other is an asking problem.

## Why this is the medical point and not the administrative one

The description is the instrument. Change in a mood presentation is not measured by an assay; it is measured by comparing two descriptions, and a description without directions, dates, and sources has no scale on it.

The concentration gap shows what that costs. Clinical practice guidelines published in 2025 record that cognitive deficits in depression persist after the episode resolves, with a documented effect on occupational and social functioning. Suppose R.'s mood lifts over the next three months and R. still cannot hold a picking list. A record that folded concentration into "depression" will show improvement while R.'s job is still at risk, and nobody will be able to see the discrepancy, because the two things were never separately described. Separating them at baseline is what makes the lag visible later.

The same logic governs the five days six years ago. Described properly, they are a dated account of a specific period from a named source, carrying the strength that a remembered episode carries: real signal, with edges soft enough that the count of days may be wrong. That is still something a future period of raised activity can be compared against. Written as "history of hypomania", the five days become an assertion that no future evidence can either confirm or dislodge.

## What this hands on

You can now describe a mood presentation across nine kinds of change, with a time course and a source on every element, and you can say what a record establishes and what it only appears to establish.

What none of that settles is what the picture is. The four-month low mood is the obvious reading, and three things sit against it: the five days six years ago that nobody assessed, the physical symptoms with no thyroid result behind them, and an alcohol pattern that is dated later than the sleep change. Weighing those against each other is the next outcome in this module, which distinguishes unipolar, bipolar-spectrum, medical, medication, substance, grief, trauma, and contextual alternatives using longitudinal evidence. It can only do that work on a description that kept its dates and its sources, which is why this lesson comes first.

Two places where this returns. When you next describe risk in a mood presentation, every element of it will need the same three tags, and "raised only on direct questioning" will be the tag that matters most. When you reach communicating and updating a formulation, the thing being updated is exactly the record you have just learned to write; a formulation built on untagged findings cannot be revised, because nobody can tell which part of it moved.

## Recovery route

If the follow-up comparison above was hard to produce, the problem is almost never the follow-up. Go back to the worked assembly and check whether each of your elements carried a direction and a source. An element that reads "poor sleep" or "low energy" will always fail this scene, and it will fail it in a real clinic in the same way. Rebuild two elements with all three tags, then return here and answer the question again for those two alone.
