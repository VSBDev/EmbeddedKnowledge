# Three things every element has to carry

A description is built out of elements. An element is one change: the sleep, the appetite, the interest in things, the ability to hold a thought. Write each one so that it carries three things, and the description survives being read by somebody who was not there.

1. **What changed**, said concretely enough that a second clinician could recognise it. "Wakes at about 04:00 and does not get back to sleep" beats "poor sleep" because it names a direction.
2. **Over what period**, which means an onset, a duration, and a pattern. "Most nights since around March" gives a pattern and an onset, and the duration follows from them. "Recently" gives none of the three.
3. **From what source**, which means one of exactly three things: R. said it, somebody else said it, or you saw it.

The third of those is the one that gets dropped, and it is the one that decides how much weight the element can bear.

## The three sources, and the thing that is not a source

| Source | What it is | How it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-report | The person's account of their own experience | Memory, mood-dependent recall, and what the person considers worth mentioning |
| Other-person report | An informant, a referral letter, a clinical record, a measurement taken elsewhere | The informant's vantage point, their stake in the account, and what they were never in a position to see |
| Observation | What you saw or heard during a bounded stretch of time | It samples minutes, and it tells you nothing about the other 23 hours |

Inference belongs on none of those rows. "Withdrawn", "guarded", "poor insight", and "labile" are conclusions about a person drawn from findings, and a record that mixes them in with findings has stopped being checkable. Keep them, use them, and mark them as yours.

Two distinctions inside that table earn their keep almost immediately.

A person's report about another person is still their report. When R. says *my mates told me I wouldn't shut up*, you have R.'s memory of a remark, not the friends' account. It sits on the self-report row, and it is weaker than the rest of that row because it is second-hand inside a first-hand account.

A measurement taken elsewhere is stronger than either party's estimate. R. thinks the weight loss is "maybe six kilos". The referring practice weighed R. at 78 kg in March and 72 kg in July. Those are the same six kilograms, arrived at two different ways, and only one of them can be compared against a number taken in three months' time.

## Time course is not decoration

An element without a time course cannot be compared with anything, including itself. Four things are worth pinning down, and none of them takes long to ask.

- **Onset.** When did this start, and did it come on over days or over months? A person who can name the week is telling you something different from a person who cannot.
- **Duration.** How long has it run without letting up?
- **Pattern.** Is it worse at a particular time of day, on particular days, or continuously?
- **Direction.** Getting worse, holding steady, or easing?

Then the part that people skip: put the dated elements next to each other. R.'s sleep changed around March. R.'s drinking increased over the last two months. The weight was 78 kg in March and 72 kg in July. R. works rotating night shifts and has done for years. Laying those on one line is description. Deciding which of them caused which is a different job, done in a later lesson, and it becomes possible only if this lesson's dates were recorded properly in the first place.

## Absent, denied, and never asked

Three states, and only the middle one is usually written down.

If R. describes hearing voices, that is present. If you ask directly and R. says no, that is a finding: **asked and denied**. If nobody raised it, that is a gap in your assessment, and it is not the same as absence.

A record that goes silent on an element is telling the next reader nothing, and the next reader will usually assume it was absent. Write the negatives you actually established, and write the gaps as gaps.

## Why the source tag is clinical and not clerical

Some things a person is well placed to report, and some things they are not. A patient's own account is the only route to what their mood feels like from inside. It is a much weaker route to a past period of raised activity, for reasons a later scene takes apart. A 2025 seminar on bipolar disorder puts the practical consequence plainly: establishing a hypomanic period from the patient's history alone is difficult, corroborative history helps confirm it, and the gap between a first diagnosis of unipolar depression and a diagnosis of bipolar disorder can run to about ten years.

That is what the source tag buys. It tells the next clinician which parts of the picture rest on the route most likely to have missed something, and where a second account would add the most.

:::{check}
:id: check-three-tags
:kind: retrieval

R. says: *"I'm tired in a way that sleeping doesn't fix."*

Write that as an element carrying all three things. You will have to notice what R. did not supply, and say so instead of inventing it.
:::

A workable version: **fatigue disproportionate to sleep, frequency and onset not established, R.'s report.** The tiredness and its quality come from R., and nothing else does, because R. was asked neither how often nor since when. Writing "for four months" there would move a number from the sleep element onto the energy element, where nobody put it. Two short questions fix it, and until they are asked the honest line is the one with the holes visible.
