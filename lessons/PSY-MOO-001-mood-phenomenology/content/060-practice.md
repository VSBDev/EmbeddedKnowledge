# Tag it, finish it, write it

Three tasks, with less help each time. Write your answers before reading the response under each one. M. and K. are invented, like everyone else in this lesson.

## 1. Tag the source

M. is 52 and drives a delivery van. These six items came out of one appointment.

1. "I've been flat since my father died in the spring."
2. M.'s daughter, who came in, says M. has not cooked a meal since June.
3. M. answered every question within a second and at normal volume.
4. "I sleep fine. Nine, ten hours. I could sleep all day."
5. The occupational health report records two absence episodes totalling eleven days since April.
6. M. seemed to be minimising.

For each item, give its source, and mark the ones that arrive without a time course.

**Response.** Items 1 and 4 are M.'s report. Item 2 is another person's report, and note what kind: the daughter is reporting an observation of her own, over months, which is a vantage point M. does not have on M.'s own cooking. Item 3 is your observation, and it is the one people forget to write down because nothing was wrong with it; a normal response latency in a person describing low mood is a finding worth recording, and it distinguishes M. from R. Item 5 is another person's record and the only item carrying a counted quantity. Item 6 is an inference, and it belongs in your impression rather than on a finding line.

Time courses: items 1, 2 and 5 state one, and item 3 carries the appointment itself as its window, which is all an observation ever has. Item 4 arrives with none, and it needs the most work, because "I sleep fine" plus ten hours plus wanting to sleep all day is a change in the opposite direction from R.'s, and nobody has yet established when it started. The question does not apply to item 6, which is a conclusion and not an element.

If you tagged item 2 simply as "collateral", go back and say what the daughter was in a position to see. An informant's value is set by their vantage point, and "has not cooked since June" is a strong item precisely because she was there for it.

## 2. Repair four defective lines

Each of these lines has one defect, and it is a different defect each time. Name the defect and rewrite the line, inventing nothing.

| | Line as written |
| --- | --- |
| a | Sleep disturbance for several months. |
| b | Has not been managing at work. |
| c | Poor insight into the severity of the illness. |
| d | *(the record says nothing at all about unusual experiences)* |

**Response.**

- **(a)** No direction. Sleeping more and sleeping less are opposite findings that this line cannot tell apart. Rewrite once the direction is known: *sleeping nine to ten hours and wanting more, since a date to be established, M.'s report.*
- **(b)** No source, and no specifics. Whose account is this? Rewrite: *two absence episodes totalling eleven days since April, occupational health record. M. has not been asked about work directly.*
- **(c)** An inference sitting on a finding line. Move it to your impression, and put the finding it rests on where the finding belongs.
- **(d)** Silence. A reader will take it as absence. If the question was asked, write the established negative. If it was not, write that it was not asked, and then ask it.

## 3. Write three elements from scratch

K. is 27 and was brought in by a housemate. This is all you have.

- The housemate says K. has been "on one" for about three weeks: awake most of the night, out of the flat before six, talking over everyone.
- K. says: "I've been getting a lot done. I've had maybe four hours a night and I don't need more."
- K. says the last two weeks have also included "crying in the mornings, for no reason".
- Observed across the appointment: speech fast and difficult to interrupt; four changes of topic in about two minutes; K. stood up twice.
- Nobody has asked K. about unusual experiences or beliefs.

Write the sleep element, the elevated element, and the functional element, each with what changed, its period, and its source. Then write the one line that records a gap.

**Response.** A workable set:

- *Sleep: about four hours a night for roughly three weeks, described by K. as sufficient. K.'s report, with the housemate independently reporting that K. is awake most of the night and out of the flat before six over the same period.*
- *Elevated: raised activity and rate of speech for about three weeks by both accounts, with reduced need for sleep and increased talkativeness. K.'s report and the housemate's report; pressured, hard-to-interrupt speech and frequent topic change observed in the appointment. K. also reports crying in the mornings over the last two weeks, so depressive and elevated features are described as running together in the last fortnight.*
- *Functional: out of the flat before six and talking over others, three weeks, housemate's report. No information about work or study.*
- *Gap: unusual experiences and beliefs not asked about.*

Two things separate a strong answer from a passable one. The first is that the reduced need for sleep is written as an element in its own right, because K. describing four hours as enough is different from K. lying awake wishing for more, and that difference is what "reduced need" names. The second is the last fortnight: crying in the mornings inside a three-week period of raised activity is a described co-occurrence, and it goes in the record as a co-occurrence with both dates. Naming what kind of episode that makes is not this lesson's task and not yet possible from this material.

If your answer named an episode type for K., go back to the previous scene and rewrite the five-days table for K.'s three weeks. If your elements came out without periods attached, redo task 1 first; the tagging habit has to be automatic before the writing gets fluent.
