## Source use and rights

Seven sources support the factual claims. All were used for facts only, followed by independent instructional structure and original expression. Complete locators, use records, rights evidence, and dated agent-access checks appear in `references.json`, and each claim's scope and uncertainty appear in `claims.json`.

- Carty and Escalona, *Brief Review of Major Depressive Disorder for Primary Care Providers*: which changes make up a major depressive episode, the two-week minimum, the requirement that they be present most days, and the spread of dysfunction.
- Singh and colleagues, *Bipolar disorder* (Lancet seminar, 2025): the descriptive features of a period of raised activity, the mania and hypomania duration conventions, psychotic features in mania and not hypomania, anxiety and agitation and irritability in mixed states, diagnostic delay, and the value of corroborative history.
- Shim and colleagues, *The prevalence and diagnostic classification of mixed features in patients with major depressive episodes*: 5.8% against 31.7% on the same 672 non-psychotic episodes depending on the criteria applied, irritable mood as the commonest elevated feature, and reclassification as anxious distress or a personality disorder.
- Rothschild, *Challenges in the Treatment of Major Depressive Disorder With Psychotic Features*: the frequency of psychotic features in depressive episodes across named populations, nihilistic delusional content, the mood-congruence distinction, and the finding that 27% of 130 diagnoses in one sample were initially wrong because the psychosis was missed. Only the epidemiological and diagnostic content was used; the paper is a treatment review and none of its treatment material appears in this lesson.
- Singh and colleagues, *Clinical practice guidelines on the assessment and management of cognitive impairment in major depressive disorder*: the affected cognitive domains, the persistence of residual deficits after an episode resolves, and the effect on occupational and social functioning. This source is displayed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, a licence this project does not adapt or redistribute, so it was used for facts only and no expression was reused.
- Regeer and colleagues, *Low self-recognition and awareness of past hypomanic and manic episodes in the general population*: the recognition and consultation percentages in 40 Dutch adults with a structured-interview bipolar diagnosis, and the explanations non-recognisers gave for those periods.
- Birk and colleagues, *Validity of retrospectively-reported depressive episodes*: that concurrent symptom scores inside later-reported episodes were higher by a medium-to-large margin, that 49.1% of within-episode reports reached the clinical cut-off, and that the recall interval did not significantly moderate the association.
